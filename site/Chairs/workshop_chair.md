# Workshop Chair Guide

Workshop chairs curate the workshop programme for conference attendees. The programme should ideally incorporate a selected variety of workshop themes and approaches, relevant to the NIME conference attendees. Workshops typically take place over one or two days before the main conference programme, on the "Workshop day(s)". There is some flexibility as to how the workshop selection might proceed, as will be discussed below. These notes are based on the experience of workshop chairs in previous editions; new chairs might choose to run the track differently, this is fine as long as the selection criteria and spirit of the track (a curated variety of workshops relevant for the NIME community) are respected. Substantial changes to the track ethos should be discussed with the general chairs and the NIME Board. Workshop documents are not included in the published proceedings; however, workshop information (such as workshop description, authors, and sometimes also the proposal document) is included on the conference website, which is archived and permanently preserved on [nime.org](https://www.nime.org).

If the workshop chairs have any questions or concerns, the point of contact are the general chairs; the local chairs are the point of contact for matters related to the venue.

## Chair responsibilities 
The responsibilities of the workshop chairs include: *(see [Tasks (detailed)](#tasks-detailed) for more specific guidelines)*
- **Directing the workshop selection process**, ensuring that the workshops comply with the selection criteria (listed below) programme is balanced and relevant to the conference attendees. This can include a peer-review process, as will be explained below.
- **Ensuring the workload is split evenly between the workshop chairs**. If one of the chairs finds themselves unable to perform their share of the task, they should communicate with their co-chair and the general chairs. Co-chairs should not be expected to take on the majority of the workload; chairs with difficulties to perform their tasks should either step down or ask the general chairs to invite an additional workshop chair to support the track coordination.
- **Coordinate the logistics of the selected workshops**: prepare a schedule and room allocation, ensure agreed technical requirements are met, coordinate workshop sign-ups, coordinate volunteers to assist workshop organisers
- **Liaising** with the workshop organisers, participants, local chairs, accessibility chairs, general chairs.
- On the workshop day(s), **assisting workshop organisers and participants on-site**, ensuring workshops are run safely and that spaces are tidied up after use.
- **After the conference ends, contributing to this guide!**

Since this is a "curated" track (curated by the chairs), there will be a conflict of interest if a workshop chair submits a workshop. As mentioned above, workshop chairs should be available on site on the workshop day(s) to address last-minute issues. 
 

## Workshop selection guidelines

There is some flexibility in how to run this track in terms of review procedure selection, either fully curated by the chairs or with the help of peer reviews. Both options will be described below; in any case chairs need to evaluate the feasibility of the proposals, in terms of scheduling, technical requirements and health and safety considerations. Workshops are also selected in terms of diversity, both thematic and in terms of organisers. 


### Selection criteria 
 It is also important to be aware of issues such as gender balance, diversity, accessibility, and any other factors that relate to the aims and ethos of NIME.
- **Relevance and quality**: The proposed theme should be relevant for the NIME community: related the community's research interests, practices, the conference year's theme, or the community itself. The relevance should be clearly articulated and typically connected with previous NIME workshops and relevant literature. 
- **Program diversity**:  Ideally the program is balanced between themes, approaches, formats, e.g. technical hands-on workshop, ideation workshops, critical/theoretical workshops, NIME community-related discussions, special interest topics, etc. If two workshops have the same theme and the proposals are equally relevant, chairs can consider suggesting a half-day format and scheduling them one after the other (i.e. as a sort of full-day thematic workshop).
- **Feasibility**: The technical requirements can be reasonably and realistically met by the organisation. This is in relation to the space and tech availability, but also in relation to the rest of the workshops, e.g., if there is only one "maker space" then only one "maker workshop" can be programmed at a time. As indicated in [Tasks (detailed)](#tasks-detailed), before starting the selection procedure, chairs should collect a list of tech and space availability so that the programme selection is done accordingly. This will likely involve liaising with the local chairs. 
- **Appropriate length**: The proposed length should be adequate for the workshop proposal. 
- **Health and safety**: some workshops might involve activities that may require previous approval by the host institution, e.g., cutting with a saw. Workshop chairs should communicate such potential "risks" to the local chairs to coordinate health and safety approval where required.
- **Compliance with the [NIME Diversity Statement](https://nime.org/diversity/)**: In the past the Statement has been interpreted as that workshop organisers can not impose any restriction on participation in terms of  ethnicity, gender, age, ability, religion, and sexual orientation. Workshops can select their participants *only* based on a call for submissions or an expression of interest related to the workshop topic. When selecting participants based on such calls, organisers must decide based on the content of such submission and not on the participant's ethnicity, gender, age, ability, religion, or sexual orientation. Workshops can of course focus on such and encourage participation of relevant groups. However, any NIME attendee should be, in principle, able to attend any workshop; the premise here is that everyone is invited to learn about any given topic at NIME. If a workshop topic is specifically on discussing experiences related to being part of a given collective, workshop organisers can then invite participants who self-identify as not part of such collectives to attend in an auditor capacity. 

#### Workshops proposing to run a research study
Workshop organisers might propose to collect data in the workshop as part of an academic study. 
- Where applicable, the study and data collection must be approved by the organiser's institutional ethics committee, receive a formal data protection assessment regulatory agency, or otherwise comply with the participant’s local institutional and national research requirements prior to commencement. 
- Studies should only be allowed if participants can attend the workshop without being obliged to taking part in the study. 
- Workshop attendees should be provided with a "participant information sheet" detailing the research study and how they will be involved if they consent, and a "consent form" where they can formally give or revoke their consent.  
- Workshop authors need to report their intentions to run a study in the proposal  and circulate the information and consent forms with attendees in advance. 
- Workshop organisers can not restrict participation to a targetted demographic or group, in accordance to the NIME Diversity Statement as indicated above. 


### Review process
  - **Non peer-reviewed route**: In some editions (e.g. 2020) this track was 'fully' curated by the workshop chairs, meaning that submissions were selected without the help of independent reviewers. Workshops then received either an 'accept' or 'reject' without further feedback unless requested by authors. This might be an appropriate route if chairs anticipate there will not be a large number of submissions and that they will be able to review all submissions personally.
  - **Single-blind peer review route**: In other editions (e.g. 2026), this track has been run as a single peer-review (i.e. non-anonymised submissions reviewed by anonymous reviewers). Workshop chairs still need to evaluate the feasibility of each workshop, so they are likely to end up reviewing all proposals anyway. Peer-reviews are useful in terms of giving feedback back to organisers (for camera-ready versions or future re-submissions). 
  - **Non peer-reviewed route (with feedback)**: However, reviewers are also generally scarce, so it is also wise to "spare" reviewers for the tracks included in proceedings such as papers or music. In NIME 2026, we "ran out" of reviewers, so we had two reviews per workshop, and then split the meta-reviews between the two co-chairs. This was quite a lot of work, and effectively would have probably been similar to following the curation route, but providing feedback to each proposal. This is time consuming but also avoids coordinating the review process.

Chairs might find that while a workshop proposal is of high quality and relevance, there is some logistical or technical issue that could be addressed with a change in the proposal. In such cases, it is adequate to give a *Conditional Accept*  with a list of required changes for the camera-ready submission.  Contact the general chairs if you have doubts. 


## Formats and conference registration policy
Workshops can either be in person or hybrid. Some editions have also allowed online-only workshops, this is to be discussed with the general chairs. Also to be discussed with them is the conference registration policy, below is the one we followed for NIME 2026:

There are a series of requirements on the registration of authors:
- At least one author must be registered for full attendance at the conference, either in person or online.
- The remaining authors must be registered either for full conference attendance (online or in person) or the workshop day(s) only.
- Online invited panelists or presenters can attend without registration. 


as well as for workshop participants:
- Workshop participants attending in person must register for either the full conference (in person) or the workshop day(s) only.
- Online participants do not need to register for the conference.


## Workshop participant signup formats 
A particularity of the workshop track is that chairs do not only need to coordinate the workshop deliveries but also their participant attendance.  

There are two **workshop sign-up modalities**, authors will be asked to choose their preferred modality in the submission form. In both cases, a participant list for each workshop will be collected. It is very important that this list includes: name of participant, modality (online or in person), email (if attending in person, this needs to be the same email as the one used to register for the conference), and accessibility requirements. 

- **Self-managed sign-ups**: Authors manage the sign-ups to their workshop. This might be because they want to select participants through a call for participation or an expression of interest. In such case, chairs will need to set a deadline for authors to return the participant list (e.g. a week before the workshop day(s)). Workshop organisers should be encouraged to share accessibility requirements as soon as possible (i.e., as soon as a person requesting adjustments signs up) so that adjustments can be arranged with the accessibility chairs. 
- **Chair-managed sign-ups**: Chairs manage the sign ups through e.g. a Google Form. It is useful to share a table with organisers where they can see the live number of participants. The form submissions should be checked regularly by the chairs so that accessibility requirements can be relayed promptly to the accessibility chairs.

Both sign up modalities should close sign ups a few days before the workshop day(s), ideally a week earlier. Ideally, thee lists are checked against the registration list so that participants who have signed up to attend a workshop in person but haven't registered to the conference can be nudged. 

If there is catering scheduled, workshop chairs should do a headcount estimate and share it with the general chairs a couple of days in advance of the workshop day(s). To estimate this headcount, chairs should consider the number of in person sign ups, as well as the number of full conference attendees. Often, many individuals with full conference registration will show up on the workshop day(s) without having registered for the conference first.  


## Example deadline calendar
This was for NIME 2026 (with peer-review):

Send out reviewer invitations – Feb 16th
Proposal submission deadline – March 5th
Send out proposals for review – March 5th
Reviews due – March 27th
Reviews released – April 3rd *(this was tight as there were reviewers who didn't complete reviews)*
Camera ready – April 30th
Chair-managed sign up form released – May 20th
Self-managed sign ups shared with chairs; chair-managed sign-up form closes; participant list shared with workshop organisers – June 20th
Workshop day –  June 23rd


## Tasks (detailed)
The below overview is just meant to serve as guidance:

- **Prepare call for participation** (September-October): The call for workshops does not usually change much between editions, but chairs should still double-check it.  See earlier call examples in the "calls" section in the left menu of this page. Edits to the call should be sent to the general chairs well in advance. The call for workshops is generally released by the general chairs and at the same time as the call for papers, music, and the rest of the tracks. 
- **Prepare submission form in the CMT** (October): NIME uses the Microsoft Conference Management Toolkit (CMT) system. See `Chairs/resources/workshop-track/cmt-forms-templates.md` for a form template. 

- **Prepare deadlines in the CMT portal**: These can be modified under the Settings panel (select settings in the top left menu); Activity Timeline menu on the left menu. The submission and camera ready deadlines should be agreed with the general chairs. 

*[Submission platform opens (usually December)]*

- If following the peer-review route, this is a good time to **plan review process**, establishing an internal deadline calendar for review and meta review, recruit reviewers. 
- Liaise with the local chairs to **assess space constraints**:  facilities, available tech, capacity of rooms. Chairs should now establish how many workshops do they have capacity to manage. 

*[Submission deadline (usually late January)]*

- **Submission selection process**: Chairs should...
  - Inspect all submissions in the CMT, **desk reject incomplete proposals** (i.e., directly assign "Reject" status in the CMT, but without releasing the decision). Proposals using a different template need not be rejected but could be conditionally accepted to ensure the authors use the correct template in the camera-ready submission; this depends on whether the proposals will be shared or not on the conference website.
  - As mentioned in the [criteria](#selection-criteria), review decisions can be "Accept", "Reject", or "Conditional Accept".
  - **Decide accept and conditional accept candidates**:
    - If following **fully curated route**: Split submissions between chairs, independently review split, then discuss what workshops to accept based on the [criteria](#selection-criteria).  Optionally chairs can provide a review for each workshop; chairs do not need to assign themselves as reviewers, reviews can be input directly from the "Submissions" panel.
    - If following **peer-review route**:
      - Assign workshops to reviewers in the CMT. See the [CMT instructions](https://cmt3.research.microsoft.com/docs/help/chair/auto-assign-reviewers.html) for automatic reviewer assignment. Ideally the "expertise" fields are used so that reviewers are matched to their preferred topics, but this is also fine to do semi-randomly (i.e. randomly first and then manually adjusting). Ideally each submission receives two reviews and a meta-review. 
        - Contact reviewers with assignment. See [CMT instructions](https://cmt3.research.microsoft.com/docs/help/chair/email-reviewers.html) for bulk emailing reviewers; you can use the provided template in `Chairs/resources/workshop-track/cmt-email-templates-reviewers.md`. 
        - Assign meta-reviewers: If  there are enough available reviewers then chairs can assign a meta-reviewer per workshop. Otherwise the chairs can act as meta-reviewers/curators; there is no need for chairs to assign themselves as meta-reviewers in the CMT as meta-reviews can be added from the "Submissions" panel.
        - *[Reviews deadline]*
        - Chase missing reviews, find last-minute reviewers for missing reviews. 
        - Chairs discuss and select based on the [criteria](#selection-criteria).
  - **Final decision considering scheduling constraints**: before confirming the review decisions, chairs should prepare a potential schedule, ensuring that they can coordinate the number of potential accepted workshops. The schedule should be done not only in accordance to the space and tech requirements available in the venue but also considering the workshop themes, i.e., ideally potentially popular workshops which would cater the same audience do not overlap. In NIME 2026 we found it easier to consider workshops either half-day (up to 4h) or full day (6-8h), and then schedule them in two slots (start set up 8am, 9am-1pm AM slot, 1-2 lunch, 2pm-6pm PM slot); full-day workshops take both the AM and PM slot. If there are many 2h proposals, chairs could consider shorter slots. Something to consider here is that ideally participants don't jump between workshops, as that could be considered disruptive by organisers. Perhaps a strategy here is to ask workshop organisers in advance whether they welcome drop-ins or not.
- **Prepare Camera-ready form**: The camera-ready form is a structured form so that authors confirm their tech requirements, max. number of participants, whether sign ups will be self-managed, etc. Although authors will have submitted a pdf, the form can be then exported as a spreadsheet which facilitates the work of the chairs. See the `Chairs/resources/workshop-track/cmt-form-templates.md`. 

*[Author notification deadline (usually early April)]*

- **Notification**: For the accepted and conditionally accepted workshops, the notification email needs to include a deadline for the camera-ready submission. Ideally the camera-ready form opens on the notification deadline. Chairs can send two emails (one for notification, one requesting the camera-ready submission) if they want to.  See the notification template emails here: `Chairs/resources/workshop-track/cmt-email-templates-reviewers.md`, and the See the [CMT instructors on how to bulk-email authors](https://cmt3.research.microsoft.com/docs/help/chair/email-authors.html) for more details. 
    - For hybrid workshops: unless the local or general chairs say otherwise, workshop organisers are responsible for creating and maintaining the online sessions, this can also be communicated in the notification email.

  

***[This is a good time to check if chairs are being able to handle workload, if it is split fairly and if it is time to bring an extra workshop chair]***

*[Camera ready deadline (usually late April)]*

- **Check camera-ready submissions**: Ensure that there is no missing info, that the submissions follow the provided template, and that the conditional accepts have addressed the acceptance conditions. If there are minor errors in the submissions that need to be amended, since the CMT does not allow reopening the Camera-ready form for individual authors, contact workshop organisers so that they send the updated PDF directly to the chairs instead of uploading it through the CMT; the chairs can then replace the old file by the updated file in the "Submissions portal".
  - It helps to export the "Submissions" table into a spreadsheet. This spreadsheet can be loaded into a platform like Google Sheets so that it can be jointly updated between the co-chairs and shared with the general chairs if necessary. 
- **Compile list of tech requirements** and liaise with local chairs to make sure everything is provided in the day. Contact workshop organisers to arrange alternatives for minor changes (major changes should have been communicated before camera-ready deadline, ideally through a conditional acceptance).

*[usually mid May, 1-1.5 months before conference]*

- **Finalise workshop program**: this will involve sending the web chairs the submission structured information (workshop number, title, abstract, organiser names, website, room number, whether sign-ups are self-managed or chair-managed).
- **Create sign-up form** for workshops with sign-ups managed with the chairs. See the NIME 2026 form example in `resources/workshop-track/managed-sign-up-form-example.pdf`. You might also include links to the calls for participation/interest of those workshops that are managing their own sign-ups. The form should collect name, modality (online or in person), email (if in person, this email has to be the same one as the one used to register for the conference; this will allow later checking whether they have registered), accessibility requirements, and comments. Ask that participants do not register for overlapping workshops. Some participants will fill up the sign-up form as a back up after submitting to a workshop with a call for participation; they should indicate this in the notes so that chairs can later correct the participant lists.  
- **Release workshop sign-up announcement** in the NIME mailing list and forum, as well as the workshop chairs' personal/academic channels. See the [NIME 2026 forum post](https://forum.nime.org/t/nime-2026-workshops-announcement-sign-up-system-calls-for-participation-and-deadlines/1059). The announcement should explain the sign-up procedure, the deadline for the managed sign-ups, and ideally include the links for the self-managed sign-ups and their internal deadlines. Deadlines should be a few days before the workshop day(s) to allow for chairs to put together the participant lists. Share the announcement with the general chairs so that it can be included in the emails sent to registered participants. 
- **Monitor the form for accessibility requirements** and share such requirements with workshop organisers and accessibility chairs so that accommodations can be put in place.  
- **Coordinate volunteers**: Volunteers might assist with the technical setup and delivery of workshops. Their role is very important to ensure the workshop day(s) run smoothly, as the chairs will not be able to stay on top of every workshop happening concurrently. If there is a "volunteer chair" coordinating volunteers, this is a good time to liaise with them. If there is no volunteer chair, workshop chairs will need to coordinate the volunteers. Depending on the number of volunteers and workshops, this might involve assigning volunteers to workshops for the setup time (from 8am for the AM/full day slot, from 1pm for the PM slot). If some workshops require more assistance, coordination may involve asking the volunteers what their preferences are, or whether they have submitted to a workshop with a participation call, and then put them in contact with the respective workshop organisers. In NIME 2026 we asked workshop organisers to communicate with their assigned volunteers when their assistance was not needed, so that they were free to attend other workshops. If possible, volunteers should sign-up through the provided channels and are given priority if a workshop is oversubscribed.


*[usually early-mid June, 2-3 weeks before conference]*

- If a half-day workshop is heavily oversubscribed, chairs might suggest the workshop runs twice, if there is space availability and it does not impede the running of other selected workshops.
- Ideally, workshops managing their own sign-ups share their participant lists with the chairs.
- Ideally, if a high volume of participants is expected, chairs check the in person sign-ups (for both chair-managed and self-managed sign-ups) against the conference registration list. Chairs can then email those who have signed up for in person workshops but haven't registered for the conference. 

*[~1 week before workshop day(s)]* 
- **Sign-ups close**
- **Email workshop organisers with participant lists as well as practical information for the workshop day(s)**: email reminder a few days before the conference gives workshop authors the chance to ask any final questions. It's also a good time to remind workshop authors about important information, such as when and where to arrive, etc. Participant name, modality and email lists are shared with workshop organisers so that they can liaise with them directly. 
  - Chairs might also want to email participants confirming their workshop choices, or share the lists with them (check with general chairs for privacy issues; sharing a list saves much more time than emailing participants individually even with template emails). 
- There will be last minute changes to sign-ups, these do not need to be so exhaustively tracked down as the point of the participant lists is to get headcount for catering (next point) and ensure people are registered; last minute show-ups non-registered can be dealt by on site.
- **If lunch is catered, estimate number of in-person attendees.** Consider it is likely that much more people will show up in the day who are registered in the conference but haven't signed up to any workshops. The estimate can be done by comparing the number of in-person sign-ups against  the in-person registration numbers. 
- Prepare signs indicating workshop schedule for each room (this might be done by the local chairs). Such signs ideally also indicate whether a workshop is full and whether it accepts drop-ins.
- Print a couple of tables indicating the schedule and room for each workshop. Even if the information is available online, attendees will ask workshop chairs for this information. Pointing attendees to such sheets is handy.

*[On the workshop day(s)]*
- Arrive early for set up time, usually 1h before the first workshop (8am?). 
- Hang up the signs indicating schedule for each room. 
- People might show up on the day without registration and not be willing to pay for the registration fee. Special cases should be discussed with the general chairs. 
- More people than those who have signed up for the workshop will show up. People will also tend to move between workshops. Clear signage indicating whether a workshop is not accepting drop-ins should help with this. 
- Some years there has been a "best workshop award", in such cases the chairs have a say or decide who to award it to. 
- At the end, chairs are responsible for ensuring workshop organisers tidy up their spaces. Chairs should also ensure all lent technical materials are put back in place or returned to their owners. Room signs should also be taken down.  

*[After the conference]*

**Please contribute to this guide after the conference ends.** The cookbook does not only help us  maintain consistency and improve the track across editions, it also eases up the workload of future chairs.
